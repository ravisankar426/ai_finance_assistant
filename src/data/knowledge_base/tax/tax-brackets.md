# Tax Brackets — Marginal vs. Effective Rates

## How brackets work
The U.S. federal income tax is **progressive**: income is taxed in layers, and each layer has its own
rate. Moving into a higher bracket only raises the rate on the dollars in that bracket — not on all
your income.

## 2026 brackets for single filers (tax year 2026)
- 10% up to **$12,400**
- 12% over $12,400; 22% over **$50,400**; 24% over **$105,700**
- 32% over **$201,775**; 35% over **$256,225**; 37% over **$640,600**

(Married filing jointly thresholds are roughly double — e.g., 22% over $100,800.)

## Standard deduction (tax year 2026)
**$16,100** for single filers, **$32,200** for married filing jointly, **$24,150** for heads of
household. Taxable income is income after deductions.

## Marginal vs. effective rate
- **Marginal rate:** the rate on your *next* dollar of income.
- **Effective rate:** total tax ÷ income.

Example: a single filer with **$60,000 of taxable income** in 2026 pays 10% on the first $12,400,
12% on the next $38,000, and 22% on the last $9,600 — about **$7,912** in total, an effective rate of
about **13.2%**, even though the marginal rate is 22%.

## Why investors care
Your marginal rate drives decisions like traditional vs. Roth contributions and the value of
deductions.
