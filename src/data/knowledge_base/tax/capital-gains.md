# Capital Gains Tax

## What a capital gain is
When you sell an investment for more than you paid (your **basis**), the profit is a capital gain;
selling for less creates a capital loss. In a taxable account, gains are taxed when you sell — not
while you hold.

## Short-term vs. long-term
- Held **one year or less:** **short-term** — taxed at your ordinary income tax rates.
- Held **more than one year:** **long-term** — taxed at lower rates of 0%, 15%, or 20%.

## Long-term rate thresholds (tax year 2025)
- **0%** up to taxable income of **$48,350** (single) or **$96,700** (married filing jointly).
- **15%** above that, up to **$533,400** (single) or **$600,050** (married filing jointly).
- **20%** above those amounts.

These thresholds are adjusted for inflation every year; check the IRS page for the year you're filing.
Some high-income investors also owe a 3.8% net investment income tax.

## Capital losses
Losses first offset gains. If losses exceed gains, you can deduct up to **$3,000** a year ($1,500 if
married filing separately) against other income, and carry the rest forward to future years.

## Ways investors manage capital gains taxes
- Holding investments more than a year.
- Using tax-advantaged accounts, where trades aren't taxed.
- Harvesting losses to offset gains (watch the wash-sale rule).
