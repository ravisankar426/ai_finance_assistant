# Traditional IRAs

## What it is
An Individual Retirement Account (IRA) you open yourself at a bank, brokerage, or other provider.
Investments grow **tax-deferred**: you pay tax when you withdraw, not each year.

## Contribution limits (tax year 2026)
For 2026, total contributions to **all** your traditional and Roth IRAs combined can't exceed
**$7,500**, or **$8,600 if you're age 50 or older** (a $1,100 catch-up). You also can't contribute
more than your taxable compensation for the year. (For 2025 the limit was $7,000, or $8,000 at 50+.)

## Is the contribution deductible?
- If neither you nor your spouse is covered by a workplace retirement plan, contributions are
  generally fully deductible.
- If you are covered, the deduction phases out above certain income levels, which the IRS adjusts
  each year.
- Nondeductible contributions are allowed; they create "basis" that isn't taxed again on withdrawal.

## Withdrawals
- Withdrawals of deductible contributions and earnings are taxed as ordinary income.
- Before age 59½, a **10% additional tax** generally applies unless an exception applies.
- Required minimum distributions generally start at age 73.

## Traditional vs. Roth in one line
Traditional: a tax break now, taxes later. Roth: taxes now, tax-free qualified withdrawals later.
