# 401(k) Plans

## What a 401(k) is
A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement plan. You contribute part of each paycheck, often with
an employer match, and the money is invested in funds offered by the plan.

## Traditional vs. Roth 401(k)
- **Traditional (pre-tax):** contributions reduce your taxable income now; withdrawals in retirement
  are taxed as ordinary income.
- **Roth 401(k):** contributions are made after tax; qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free.

## 2026 contribution limits (tax year 2026)
- Employee elective deferrals: **$24,500**.
- Catch-up contributions for age 50 and older: **$8,000** (traditional and safe harbor 401(k) plans).
- Higher catch-up for ages **60–63**: **$11,250**.
- Total of employee and employer contributions: **$72,000** ($80,000 including age-50 catch-up, or
  up to $83,250 for ages 60–63).

These limits are adjusted for inflation most years; check the IRS page for the current year.

## Withdrawals
Withdrawals before age 59½ are generally taxable and subject to a **10% additional tax** unless an
exception applies. Required minimum distributions generally start at age 73 for traditional
balances.

## Good to know
- Employer contributions may vest over time.
- Many plans allow loans, but unpaid loans can become taxable distributions.
- When you change jobs, you can usually leave the money, roll it to the new plan, or roll it to an IRA.
