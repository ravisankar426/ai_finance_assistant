# The Wash-Sale Rule

## The rule
A wash sale happens when you sell stock or securities at a loss and, **within 30 days before or after
the sale**, you buy (or get a contract or option to buy) **substantially identical** stock or
securities. That's a 61-day window around the sale date.

## What happens to the loss
The loss isn't deductible at the time of the sale. Instead, the disallowed loss is **added to the
cost basis of the new shares**, which postpones the deduction until you sell the new shares. The
holding period of the old shares also carries over to the new ones.

## Example
You buy 100 shares for $1,000, sell them for $800 (a $200 loss), and buy 100 shares of the same stock
two weeks later for $820. The $200 loss is disallowed; your basis in the new shares becomes $1,020
($820 + $200).

## Common traps
- Buying the same security in another account you control, including an IRA, can trigger the rule.
- Automatic dividend reinvestment within the window can create a partial wash sale.

## "Substantially identical"
Shares of the same company are clearly identical. Two funds tracking the same index may be treated as
substantially identical; funds tracking different indexes generally are not, but the IRS hasn't
drawn a precise line.
