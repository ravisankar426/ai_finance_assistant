# Tax-Loss Harvesting

## The idea
Tax-loss harvesting means selling an investment in a taxable account that's below what you paid, to
realize a capital loss that can reduce your taxes. You can then reinvest the money to stay invested.

## How the loss helps
- Capital losses first offset capital gains.
- If losses exceed gains, up to **$3,000** a year ($1,500 married filing separately) can offset other
  income.
- Unused losses carry forward indefinitely to future years.

## The wash-sale rule
If you buy **substantially identical** securities within 30 days before or after the sale, the loss is
disallowed for now (added to the basis of the new shares). Investors who harvest losses often switch
to a similar but not substantially identical fund to stay invested.

## Things to weigh
- It doesn't erase taxes forever: lowering your basis can mean a larger gain later. The benefit comes
  from deferral, and from offsetting higher-taxed gains or income.
- Transaction costs and bid-ask spreads.
- It only applies to **taxable accounts** — losses inside IRAs and 401(k)s can't be harvested.

## Who benefits most
Investors with realized gains to offset, or in higher tax brackets, during market declines.
