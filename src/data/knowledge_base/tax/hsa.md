# Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)

## Eligibility
You can contribute to an HSA if you're covered by a **high-deductible health plan (HDHP)** and have
no other disqualifying health coverage (and aren't enrolled in Medicare or claimed as someone's
dependent).

## The triple tax advantage
1. Contributions are tax-deductible (or pre-tax through payroll).
2. Growth is tax-free.
3. Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.

## Contribution limits (tax year 2026)
- Self-only HDHP coverage: **$4,400**.
- Family HDHP coverage: **$8,750**.
- Catch-up contribution if you're **55 or older**: an extra **$1,000**.

## Using an HSA for retirement
Unused balances roll over year to year and stay yours if you change jobs. Many HSAs let you invest
the balance. After age 65, withdrawals for non-medical expenses are taxed as ordinary income but
aren't penalized — similar to a traditional IRA — while medical withdrawals remain tax-free.

## Watch out
Non-medical withdrawals before 65 are taxable and generally face an additional 20% tax. Keep receipts
for medical expenses you pay from the account.
