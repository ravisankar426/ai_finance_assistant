# 529 Education Savings Plans

## What a 529 plan is
A 529 plan (qualified tuition program) is a tax-advantaged account for education costs, sponsored by
states or educational institutions. There's no federal deduction for contributions, but some states
offer a state income-tax deduction or credit.

## Tax benefits
Earnings grow tax-free, and withdrawals are tax-free when used for **qualified education expenses**
of the beneficiary — such as tuition, fees, books, and room and board at eligible institutions. K-12
tuition is also allowed up to an annual limit set by law.

## Non-qualified withdrawals
If money is withdrawn for other purposes, the **earnings** portion is generally subject to income tax
plus a **10% additional tax** (with exceptions, such as when the beneficiary receives a scholarship).

## Rolling leftover money to a Roth IRA
Since 2024, a beneficiary can roll unused 529 money into their own Roth IRA, subject to conditions:
- The 529 account must have been open for **more than 15 years**.
- Each year's rollover can't exceed the annual Roth IRA contribution limit.
- Lifetime total rollovers are capped at **$35,000**.
- Contributions made within the last 5 years (and their earnings) aren't eligible.

## Other features
- You can usually change the beneficiary to another family member.
- Plans offer age-based portfolios that become more conservative as the student nears college.
