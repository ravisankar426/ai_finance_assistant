# Qualified vs. Ordinary Dividends

## Two tax treatments
- **Qualified dividends** are taxed at the lower long-term capital gains rates (0%, 15%, or 20%).
- **Ordinary (non-qualified) dividends** are taxed at your regular income tax rates.

## What makes a dividend qualified
Generally, the dividend must be paid by a U.S. corporation or a qualified foreign corporation, and
you must meet a **holding period**: you must have held the stock for **more than 60 days during the
121-day period that begins 60 days before the ex-dividend date**.

## Dividends that usually aren't qualified
- Most REIT dividends.
- Dividends on shares held too briefly to meet the holding period.
- Distributions from money market funds and many bond funds (which are largely interest).

## Where you'll see it
Your broker's Form 1099-DIV separates total ordinary dividends from the qualified portion.

## Why it matters
For a long-term investor holding dividend-paying stock funds in a taxable account, most dividends are
often qualified — a meaningful tax difference. In IRAs and 401(k)s the distinction doesn't apply
while the money stays in the account.
