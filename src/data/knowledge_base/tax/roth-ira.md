# Roth IRAs

## How a Roth IRA works
You contribute money you've already paid tax on. Investments grow tax-free, and **qualified
withdrawals in retirement are tax-free**. There's no tax deduction for contributions.

## Contribution limit (tax year 2026)
Combined traditional + Roth IRA contributions are limited to **$7,500** for 2026 (**$8,600** at age
50+), and no more than your taxable compensation.

## Income limits (tax year 2026)
Your ability to contribute phases out at higher incomes (modified adjusted gross income):
- **Single and head of household:** phase-out between **$153,000 and $168,000**.
- **Married filing jointly:** phase-out between **$242,000 and $252,000**.

Above the top of the range you can't contribute directly to a Roth IRA.

## Withdrawals
- Your **contributions** can generally be withdrawn at any time without tax or penalty.
- **Earnings** are tax-free if the withdrawal is "qualified": generally after age 59½ and at least 5
  years after your first Roth IRA contribution (other qualifying events exist).
- Roth IRAs have **no required minimum distributions** during the original owner's lifetime.

## Who often considers a Roth
People who expect to be in a similar or higher tax bracket in retirement, younger savers with many
years of tax-free growth ahead, and anyone wanting tax diversification.
