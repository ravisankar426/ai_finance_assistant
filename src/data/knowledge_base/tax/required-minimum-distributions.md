# Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

## What RMDs are
Tax-deferred retirement accounts can't grow untaxed forever. Required minimum distributions are the
minimum amounts you must withdraw each year once you reach a certain age.

## When they start
You generally must start taking withdrawals from traditional IRAs, SEP IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs, and
retirement plan accounts when you reach **age 73**. Your first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of
the year after you turn 73, but then you'll take two RMDs in that year.

## How much
Each year's RMD is generally your account balance at the end of the previous year divided by a
life-expectancy factor from IRS tables. RMDs are taxed as ordinary income (except for any
nondeductible basis).

## Roth accounts
**Roth IRAs** and designated Roth accounts in 401(k) and 403(b) plans don't require withdrawals while
the original owner is alive.

## Missing an RMD
The amount not withdrawn may be subject to an excise tax of **25%**, reduced to **10%** if the RMD is
corrected in a timely manner (generally within two years).

## Planning ideas
Some retirees convert part of traditional balances to Roth before RMDs begin, or give RMD amounts
directly to charity through qualified charitable distributions — both have rules worth reviewing
with a tax professional.
