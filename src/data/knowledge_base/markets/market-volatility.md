# Handling Market Volatility

## What volatility feels like
Markets sometimes swing sharply for days or weeks. Headlines get louder, and the urge to "do
something" grows.

## Don't panic — plan
- **Revisit your plan, not the headlines.** Most investment plans are built for the long term.
- **Avoid trying to time the market.** Selling to "wait it out" means guessing both when to get out
  and when to get back in.
- **Diversify.** A mix of stocks, bonds, and cash reduces the impact of any single market's swings.
- **Know your risk tolerance** — and if a downturn shows you took on too much risk, adjust thoughtfully
  rather than in a panic.
- **Keep an emergency fund** so you don't have to sell investments at low prices.
- **Keep contributing.** Regular investing buys more shares when prices are lower; keep capturing any
  employer match.

## Market-wide circuit breakers
U.S. exchanges have circuit breakers that temporarily halt trading if the S&P 500 falls by set
percentages in a single day, giving investors time to absorb information.

## Beware of opportunists
Volatile periods attract scams promising safety or quick recovery. Be skeptical of guaranteed-return
pitches.

## Takeaway
Volatility is the price of long-term growth potential; a plan you can stick with is the best defense.
