# Bull and Bear Markets

## Definitions
- **Bear market:** a time when stock prices are declining and sentiment is pessimistic. Generally, a
  bear market occurs when a broad market index falls by **20% or more over at least a two-month
  period**.
- **Bull market:** a time when prices are rising and sentiment is optimistic.
- **Correction:** a smaller decline — commonly described as about 10% from a recent high.

## They're a normal part of investing
Bear markets have happened many times in U.S. history, and broad markets have historically recovered
and gone on to new highs — though recoveries have taken months in some cases and years in others.
Nobody can reliably predict when one will start or end.

## What tends to go wrong in bear markets
- **Panic selling:** selling after a big drop turns a temporary decline into a permanent loss and risks
  missing the rebound, which often comes unexpectedly.
- **Taking more risk than you can stomach** in the preceding bull market.

## What investors often do instead
- Keep an emergency fund so they don't need to sell investments at low prices.
- Stick with a long-term plan and allocation; rebalance.
- Keep contributing regularly — dollar-cost averaging buys more shares at lower prices.

## Takeaway
Plan for bear markets before they happen by choosing an allocation you can hold through one.
