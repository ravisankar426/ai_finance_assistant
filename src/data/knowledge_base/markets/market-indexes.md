# Market Indexes — S&P 500, Dow, Nasdaq

## What an index is
A market index tracks the performance of a group of securities, giving a snapshot of a market or a
segment of it. You can't invest in an index directly, but index funds and ETFs aim to match one.

## The best-known U.S. stock indexes
- **S&P 500:** about 500 large U.S. companies, weighted by market value. Widely used as the benchmark
  for "the U.S. stock market."
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average (the Dow):** 30 large U.S. companies, weighted by share price — an
  older, narrower measure.
- **Nasdaq Composite:** most stocks listed on the Nasdaq exchange, heavily weighted toward technology.
  The **Nasdaq-100** tracks the 100 largest non-financial Nasdaq companies.
- **Russell 2000:** about 2,000 smaller U.S. companies — a common small-cap benchmark.

## ETFs that track them
This assistant's market overview uses popular ETFs as stand-ins: **SPY** (S&P 500), **QQQ**
(Nasdaq-100), **DIA** (Dow), and **IWM** (Russell 2000).

## Weighting matters
In a market-value-weighted index, the largest companies move the index the most; a few giant
companies can drive a large share of its returns.

## Using indexes
Indexes are benchmarks for judging performance: a fund's return is usually compared with the index
that matches its strategy.
