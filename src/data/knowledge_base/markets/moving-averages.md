# Moving Averages and Price Trends

## What a moving average is
A moving average smooths out daily price noise by averaging prices over a rolling window. Each day,
the oldest price drops out and the newest is added, so the average "moves."
- **Simple moving average (SMA):** the plain average of the last N closing prices.
- **Exponential moving average (EMA):** weights recent prices more heavily, reacting faster.

## The 50-day and 200-day averages
- The **50-day** average follows recent price action and is used to describe short- to medium-term
  trends.
- The **200-day** average describes the long-term trend.
A price above its 200-day average is often described as being in a long-term uptrend; below it, a
downtrend.

## Crossovers
Technical analysts watch when a shorter average crosses a longer one: the 50-day crossing **above**
the 200-day is called a "golden cross," and crossing **below** a "death cross."

## Limits
Moving averages are **backward-looking** — they describe what prices have done, not what they will
do. Crossover signals are often late and produce false signals in choppy markets. They describe
trends; they don't predict prices.
