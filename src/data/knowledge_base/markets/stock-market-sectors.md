# Stock Market Sectors

## The 11 GICS sectors
Companies are commonly grouped by the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS), developed by
MSCI and S&P Dow Jones Indices. It has 11 sectors:
1. **Energy** — oil, gas, coal, and energy equipment.
2. **Materials** — chemicals, construction materials, metals and mining, packaging.
3. **Industrials** — aerospace and defense, machinery, construction, transportation, business services.
4. **Consumer Discretionary** — autos, retailers, hotels, restaurants, leisure products.
5. **Consumer Staples** — food, beverages, household and personal products.
6. **Health Care** — pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, medical devices, health services.
7. **Financials** — banks, insurance, capital markets, consumer finance.
8. **Information Technology** — software, hardware, semiconductors.
9. **Communication Services** — telecom, media, entertainment, interactive platforms.
10. **Utilities** — electric, gas, and water utilities.
11. **Real Estate** — real-estate companies and equity REITs.

## Cyclical vs. defensive
- **Cyclical** sectors (consumer discretionary, industrials, materials, financials) tend to swing more
  with the economy.
- **Defensive** sectors (consumer staples, health care, utilities) tend to be steadier in downturns.

## Why sectors matter
Sector exposure is a source of concentration risk: a portfolio full of technology stocks moves with
that one sector. This assistant shows sector allocation for portfolios and tracks sector ETFs in the
market overview.
