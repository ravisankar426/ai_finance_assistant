# Dividends

## What a dividend is
A dividend is a distribution of a company's earnings to its shareholders, usually paid in cash on a
regular schedule (often quarterly). Funds also pass along the dividends and interest they receive.
Companies are not required to pay dividends, and they can reduce or stop them at any time.

## Key dates
- **Declaration date:** the company announces the dividend.
- **Ex-dividend date:** you must own the shares **before** this date to receive the dividend.
- **Payment date:** the dividend is paid.

## Dividend yield
Dividend yield = annual dividends per share ÷ share price. A stock paying $2 a year at a $50 price
yields 4%. A very high yield can be a warning sign: it may mean the price fell because investors
expect the dividend to be cut.

## Reinvesting
Many brokers and funds offer **dividend reinvestment**, using dividends to buy more shares
automatically. Reinvesting lets returns compound over time.

## Taxes
In a taxable account, dividends are generally taxable in the year received, even if reinvested.
"Qualified" dividends are taxed at the lower long-term capital gains rates; others are taxed as
ordinary income. In tax-advantaged accounts such as IRAs and 401(k)s, dividends are not taxed when
paid.

## Total return
A stock's or fund's **total return** combines price change and dividends; looking at price alone
understates the return of dividend-paying investments.
