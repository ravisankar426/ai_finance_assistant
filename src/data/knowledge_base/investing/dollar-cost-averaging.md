# Dollar-Cost Averaging

## What it is
Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals — for example,
$200 every month — regardless of whether prices are up or down. Contributing to a workplace
retirement plan from every paycheck is a common example.

## How it works
Because the amount is fixed, you automatically buy **more shares when prices are low** and **fewer
shares when prices are high**. Example with $100 a month:

| Month | Price per share | Shares bought |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $10 | 10.0 |
| 2 | $8 | 12.5 |
| 3 | $12.50 | 8.0 |

You invested $300 for 30.5 shares, an average cost of about $9.84 per share — below the simple
average price of $10.17.

## Benefits
- **Removes the need to time the market**, which even professionals struggle to do.
- **Builds a habit** and makes investing automatic.
- **Reduces regret** from investing a large sum right before a drop.

## Limitations
- It does **not guarantee a profit or protect against losses** in a declining market.
- If you already have a lump sum, investing it all at once has historically come out ahead more
  often than spreading it out, because markets have tended to rise over time — but spreading it out
  can feel more comfortable.
- Each purchase may involve transaction costs at some brokers.
