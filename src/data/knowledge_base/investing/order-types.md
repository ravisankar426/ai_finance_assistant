# Types of Stock Orders

## Market order
Buy or sell **immediately at the best available price**. It almost always executes, but the price
isn't guaranteed — in fast-moving or thinly traded markets, the price you get can differ from the
last quote you saw.

## Limit order
Buy or sell only **at a specified price or better** (a buy limit at or below your price; a sell limit
at or above it). You control the price, but the order may never execute if the market doesn't reach
your limit.

## Stop order (stop-loss)
Becomes a **market order once the stock trades at your stop price**. Often used to limit losses. In a
sharp drop, the sale can execute well below the stop price.

## Stop-limit order
Becomes a **limit order** once the stop price is reached — you control the price, but the order may
not execute if the price moves past your limit.

## Time in force
- **Day order:** expires at the end of the trading day if not filled.
- **Good-'til-canceled (GTC):** stays open until filled or canceled (brokers often set a maximum).

## Practical tips
- Long-term investors buying widely traded funds often use limit orders near the current price to
  avoid surprises.
- Trading outside regular hours can mean wider bid-ask spreads and bigger price swings.
