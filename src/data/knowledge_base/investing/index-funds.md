# Index Funds

## What an index fund is
An index fund is a mutual fund or ETF that aims to match the performance of a market index — such
as the S&P 500, which tracks about 500 large U.S. companies — by holding the same securities (or a
representative sample). Instead of a manager picking investments, the fund simply follows the index.
This is called **passive investing**.

## Why many investors use them
- **Broad diversification:** one fund can hold hundreds or thousands of companies, reducing the
  impact of any single company doing badly.
- **Low costs:** because there is no active stock-picking, expense ratios are typically low. Over
  decades, lower fees can mean noticeably more money stays invested and compounds.
- **Simplicity and transparency:** you know what the fund aims to hold.

## What to keep in mind
- An index fund follows its index **down as well as up**; it does not try to avoid market declines.
- It will slightly **trail** the index because of fees and small tracking differences.
- Different index funds track very different things (large U.S. companies, small companies, bonds,
  international stocks), so their risk levels differ a lot.

## Active vs. passive
Actively managed funds try to beat the market by selecting investments, usually at higher cost.
Many actively managed funds fail to beat their benchmark index over long periods after fees, which
is one reason index funds are popular with long-term investors.

## Questions to ask before choosing one
What index does it track? What is the expense ratio? Does it fit your goals and time horizon?
