# Target Date Funds

## What they are
A target date fund is a mutual fund or ETF built for investors who plan to retire (or reach another
goal) around a specific year — named in the fund, such as "2055." It holds a diversified mix of stock
and bond funds and **gradually shifts toward a more conservative mix** as the target year approaches.

## The glide path
The schedule by which the fund changes its stock/bond mix over time is called its **glide path**.
Some glide paths keep changing for years after the target date ("through" funds); others reach their
most conservative mix at the target date ("to" funds).

## Why people use them
- One-fund simplicity: diversification and rebalancing happen automatically.
- They're common default options in workplace retirement plans.

## What to check
- **Different funds with the same year can be very different** — in stock exposure, holdings, and
  glide paths — and so in risk and returns.
- **Fees:** costs vary widely between fund families.
- **No guarantee:** target date funds can lose money, including near or after the target date, and
  don't guarantee enough retirement income.
- **Overlap:** holding a target date fund plus other funds can unintentionally change your overall
  allocation.

## Takeaway
Pick a fund based on its glide path, holdings, and fees — not just the year in its name.
