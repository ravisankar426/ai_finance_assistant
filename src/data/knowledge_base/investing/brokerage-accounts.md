# Opening a Brokerage Account

## What a brokerage account is
A brokerage account lets you buy and sell investments — stocks, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds — through
a registered broker-dealer. Unlike retirement accounts, a regular (taxable) brokerage account has no
contribution limits or withdrawal restrictions, but earnings are taxable each year.

## What you'll be asked for
Brokers are required to collect identifying and financial information: your name, address, date of
birth, tax ID (such as a Social Security number), employment, income, net worth, investment
experience, and investment objectives. This helps them comply with regulations and recommend
suitable investments.

## Cash vs. margin accounts
- **Cash account:** you pay the full price of what you buy.
- **Margin account:** you can borrow from the broker to buy securities, which magnifies both gains
  and losses and can force sales if prices fall. Some applications make margin the default, so
  confirm which account type you are opening.

## Fees to ask about
Trading commissions (many brokers charge none for online stock and ETF trades), account maintenance
fees, transfer fees, and fees for paper statements or special services.

## Protection if a broker fails
SIPC (Securities Investor Protection Corporation) coverage protects customers if a member brokerage
firm fails and assets are missing — up to $500,000, including up to $250,000 for cash. It does **not**
protect against investment losses from market declines.

## Check the firm
Look up the broker on Investor.gov or FINRA BrokerCheck before opening an account.
