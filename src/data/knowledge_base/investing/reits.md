# Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

## What a REIT is
A REIT is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate — apartments,
offices, warehouses, shopping centers, data centers, cell towers, or mortgages. REITs let individuals
invest in large-scale real estate without buying property directly.

## Why they pay high dividends
To qualify as a REIT, a company must generally distribute at least 90% of its taxable income to
shareholders as dividends. As a result, REITs are often used for income.

## Types
- **Publicly traded REITs:** listed on an exchange; easy to buy and sell, with prices that move daily.
- **Non-traded REITs:** registered with the SEC but not listed. They can be hard to sell, may have
  high upfront fees, and are difficult to value. Investors should be especially cautious.
- **Private REITs:** not registered; generally available only to certain institutional or
  high-net-worth investors.

## Risks
- Sensitive to interest rates and the real-estate cycle.
- Dividends are not guaranteed and can be cut.
- Most REIT dividends are taxed as ordinary income rather than at qualified-dividend rates (unless
  held in a tax-advantaged account).

## Easy diversification
Many investors get real-estate exposure through REIT index funds or ETFs instead of picking
individual REITs.
