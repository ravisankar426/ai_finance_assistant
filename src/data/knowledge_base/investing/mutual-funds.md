# Mutual Funds

## What a mutual fund is
A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in a portfolio of stocks, bonds, or
other securities, managed by a professional investment adviser. Each share represents a slice of the
whole portfolio.

## How you buy and sell
Mutual fund shares are bought from and sold back to the fund (directly or through a broker) at the
fund's **net asset value (NAV)**, calculated once per trading day after the market closes. Unlike
ETFs, they don't trade throughout the day.

## Types
- **Stock (equity) funds**, **bond funds**, **money market funds**, and **balanced funds** that hold
  a mix.
- **Index funds** that track a market index, and **actively managed funds** whose managers try to
  beat one.
- **Target date funds** that shift their mix over time toward a target year.

## Benefits
Diversification, professional management, and convenience — including automatic reinvestment of
dividends and small regular investments.

## Costs and risks
- **Expense ratio:** an annual fee taken from fund assets. Some funds also have **sales loads**
  (commissions) or other fees. Fees reduce your returns every year.
- **Market risk:** the fund's value rises and falls with its holdings; mutual funds are not insured.
- **Taxes:** funds distribute capital gains and dividends, which can be taxable in a regular
  brokerage account even if you reinvest them.

## Before investing
Read the fund's prospectus or summary prospectus for its objective, holdings, risks, and fees.
