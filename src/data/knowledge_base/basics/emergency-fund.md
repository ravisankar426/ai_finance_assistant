# Emergency Fund

## What it is
An emergency fund is cash set aside for unexpected expenses or income loss — a car repair, a
medical bill, or a job loss. It keeps a surprise expense from turning into high-interest debt or
forcing you to sell investments at a bad time.

## How much to save
There is no single right number. A common guideline is **three to six months of essential living
expenses**, with more if your income is irregular or you support others. If that feels out of
reach, start smaller: even a few hundred dollars covers many common emergencies. Saving a small,
regular amount automatically is often easier than saving a large lump sum.

## Where to keep it
The money should be **safe and easy to reach**, not invested in stocks that might be down when you
need it. Common choices are a savings account or money market deposit account at a bank or credit
union insured by the FDIC or NCUA.

## Emergency fund vs. investing
Many people build at least a starter emergency fund before investing for long-term goals, so that
a surprise expense doesn't force them to sell investments at a loss. After using the fund, the goal
is to rebuild it.

## Tips to build one
- Set a specific target and track progress.
- Automate a transfer on payday.
- Save windfalls such as tax refunds or bonuses.
