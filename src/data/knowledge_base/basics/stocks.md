# Stocks

## What a stock is
A stock (also called a share or equity) is a small piece of ownership in a company. When you buy
a share, you own a fraction of that company's assets and future profits. Companies sell stock to
raise money to grow their business.

## How stock investors can make money
- **Price appreciation:** if the company grows and investors value it more, the share price can
  rise, and you can sell for more than you paid.
- **Dividends:** some companies pay part of their profits to shareholders as cash dividends,
  typically every quarter. Dividends are never guaranteed and can be cut.

## The risks
Stock prices can fall as well as rise, sometimes sharply, and you can lose some or all of the money
you invest. A single company can struggle or go bankrupt. Prices also move with the overall market,
the economy, interest rates, and investor sentiment. Historically stocks have offered higher
long-term returns than bonds or cash, but with much larger short-term swings.

## Common stock vs. preferred stock
- **Common stock** usually gives voting rights and a share of profits through dividends and growth.
- **Preferred stock** usually pays fixed dividends that must be paid before common dividends, but
  typically has no voting rights and less upside.

## Key terms
- **Ticker symbol:** a short code that identifies a stock on an exchange (for example, AAPL).
- **Market capitalization:** share price times shares outstanding — the market's total value of
  the company.
- **Exchange:** a regulated marketplace where stocks are bought and sold, such as the NYSE or Nasdaq.

## Takeaway for beginners
Owning one or a few individual stocks concentrates your risk. Many beginners get stock exposure
through diversified funds (such as index funds) instead of picking individual companies.
