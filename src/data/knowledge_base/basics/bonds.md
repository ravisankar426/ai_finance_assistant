# Bonds

## What a bond is
A bond is a loan you make to a government or company. In return, the issuer promises to pay you
interest (the **coupon**) on a schedule and to repay the original amount (the **principal** or
**face value**) on a set date (the **maturity date**). Bonds are often called fixed-income
investments because the interest payments are usually set in advance.

## Common types
- **U.S. Treasury securities:** issued by the federal government; considered to have very low
  default risk.
- **Municipal bonds:** issued by states and cities; interest may be exempt from federal income tax.
- **Corporate bonds:** issued by companies; usually pay more interest than Treasuries to compensate
  for higher risk.

## The main risks
- **Interest-rate risk:** bond prices move in the opposite direction of interest rates. If rates
  rise, existing bonds paying lower rates become less valuable, so their market price falls.
  Longer-maturity bonds are more sensitive to rate changes.
- **Credit (default) risk:** the issuer might fail to pay interest or principal. Credit rating
  agencies grade issuers; lower-rated ("high-yield" or "junk") bonds pay more because they are riskier.
- **Inflation risk:** fixed payments buy less if prices rise faster than the bond's interest rate.

## Why investors hold bonds
Bonds generally fluctuate less than stocks and provide regular income. In a diversified portfolio
they can cushion stock-market declines, though they usually offer lower long-term returns than stocks.

## Key terms
- **Yield:** the return you earn on a bond, which depends on the price you pay as well as the coupon.
- **Maturity:** when the principal is repaid; can range from months to 30 years or more.
