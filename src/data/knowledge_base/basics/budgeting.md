# Budgeting Basics

## Why budget
A budget is a plan for your money: how much comes in, where it goes, and what is left for saving and
investing. Investing usually starts with a budget, because you can only invest money you don't need
for current bills and emergencies.

## How to build one
1. **List your income** — take-home pay and any other regular income.
2. **Track your spending** for a typical month — fixed costs (rent, loan payments, insurance) and
   variable costs (groceries, transportation, entertainment).
3. **Compare** income with spending to see what is left over or where you overspend.
4. **Set priorities:** essentials first, then savings goals, then wants.
5. **Review regularly** and adjust when your income or expenses change.

## Needs vs. wants
Separating needs (housing, food, utilities, minimum debt payments) from wants (dining out,
subscriptions) makes it easier to find money for saving. Some people use rules of thumb such as
dividing take-home pay into needs, wants, and savings, but any plan that you can stick with works.

## Pay yourself first
Treat saving like a bill: set up an automatic transfer to savings or an investment account on payday,
so saving happens before spending.

## Common pitfalls
- Forgetting irregular expenses (car repairs, annual fees, gifts) — set aside a little each month.
- Making the budget too strict to follow.
- Not revisiting it after a raise, a move, or a new expense.
