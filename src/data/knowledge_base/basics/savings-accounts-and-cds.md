# Savings Accounts and Certificates of Deposit (CDs)

## Savings and money market deposit accounts
Bank savings accounts and money market deposit accounts hold cash safely and let you withdraw it
when needed. They are good homes for an emergency fund or money needed within a few years. Interest
rates vary by bank and change over time.

## Certificates of deposit (CDs)
A CD is a deposit you agree to leave at a bank for a fixed term — for example, 6 months or 5 years —
usually in exchange for a fixed interest rate. If you withdraw early, you typically pay an
**early-withdrawal penalty**. Some CDs sold through brokers ("brokered CDs") can be sold before
maturity, but their market price can fall if interest rates rise.

## Deposit insurance
Deposits at FDIC-insured banks (or NCUA-insured credit unions) are protected up to **$250,000 per
depositor, per insured institution, for each account ownership category**. That protection covers
bank failure — it does not apply to stocks, bonds, mutual funds, or crypto assets, even if bought
through a bank.

## Trade-offs
- **Pros:** principal protection (within insurance limits), predictable returns, simplicity.
- **Cons:** returns may trail inflation over long periods, and CDs lock up money for their term.

## Where they fit
Savings accounts and CDs suit short-term goals and emergency money. Long-term goals usually need some
investments with growth potential to outpace inflation.
