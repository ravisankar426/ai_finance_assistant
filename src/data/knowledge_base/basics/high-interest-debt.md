# High-Interest Debt vs. Investing

## The core idea
Paying off a debt is like earning a guaranteed return equal to its interest rate. Credit card
interest rates are often far higher than the long-term returns investors can reasonably expect, and
unlike investment returns they are certain. That is why many educators suggest paying down
high-interest debt before investing beyond what is needed to capture any employer retirement match.

## How credit card interest works
If you don't pay your full statement balance, interest is charged on the remaining balance, and it
compounds. Paying only the minimum can keep you in debt for years and cost far more than the original
purchases.

## A common order of priorities
1. Build a small starter emergency fund, so a surprise expense doesn't go on a card.
2. Contribute enough to a workplace retirement plan to get the full employer match, if offered.
3. Pay down high-interest debt aggressively.
4. Grow the emergency fund and invest for longer-term goals.

## Strategies for paying debt down
- **Avalanche:** pay extra on the highest-interest debt first — saves the most interest.
- **Snowball:** pay extra on the smallest balance first — quick wins that build motivation.

## Lower-interest debt
Debts with low, fixed rates (such as some mortgages or student loans) are a different decision;
people often invest while paying them on schedule. The right balance depends on the rate, your risk
tolerance, and your goals.
