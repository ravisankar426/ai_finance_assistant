# Red Flags of Investment Fraud

## Warning signs
- **Guaranteed high returns with little or no risk.** Every investment carries risk; higher potential
  returns come with higher risk. "Risk-free" big returns are a classic sign of fraud.
- **Pressure to act now.** Legitimate professionals don't push you to decide immediately.
- **"Everyone is buying it."** Fraudsters use social proof and fake testimonials.
- **Unlicensed sellers** or exaggerated credentials.
- **Unsolicited offers** — calls, texts, social-media messages, or online "friends" who bring up
  investing, including crypto trading opportunities.
- **Unusual payment methods:** gift cards, wiring money overseas, peer-to-peer payment apps, crypto
  assets, or paying an individual instead of a firm.

## How to protect yourself
1. **Ask questions** and get answers in writing.
2. **Research** the investment — is it registered? Can you find independent information about it?
3. **Check the seller's background** using the free search tool on Investor.gov (and FINRA
   BrokerCheck).
4. Be skeptical of anyone who discourages you from getting a second opinion.

## If something seems wrong
You can report suspected fraud to the SEC through its online tips portal, or contact FINRA or your
state securities regulator.

## A note on AI and online scams
Scammers increasingly use AI-generated content, fake websites, and impersonations of real firms and
professionals. Verify identities through official channels before sending money.
