# Rebalancing a Portfolio

## Why portfolios drift
You set a target mix — say 70% stocks and 30% bonds. As markets move, the faster-growing part takes
a bigger share: after a strong year for stocks you might be at 78/22, carrying more risk than you
planned.

## What rebalancing means
Rebalancing brings the portfolio back to its target mix by selling some of what has grown and buying
what has lagged — or, more gently, by directing new contributions to the underweight assets.

## Common approaches
- **Calendar:** rebalance on a schedule, such as once a year.
- **Threshold:** rebalance when an asset class drifts more than a set amount (for example, 5
  percentage points) from its target.
- **Contribution-based:** use new money to fix the drift, avoiding sales.

## Things to keep in mind
- **Taxes:** selling in a taxable account can create capital gains. Rebalancing inside retirement
  accounts, or with new contributions, avoids that.
- **Costs:** trading fees and bid-ask spreads add up if you rebalance too often.
- **Discipline:** rebalancing means buying what has recently done worse, which can feel
  uncomfortable — that's part of why it controls risk.

## Automatic options
Target date funds, balanced funds, and robo-advisers rebalance for you.
