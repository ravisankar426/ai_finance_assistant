# International Diversification

## Why invest internationally
Most of the world's companies — and a large share of global stock-market value — are outside the
U.S. International stocks and bonds don't always move in step with U.S. markets, so adding them can
spread risk and give access to growth in other economies.

## Ways to invest
- **International and global mutual funds or ETFs** — the simplest route for most individuals.
- **Developed-market vs. emerging-market funds:** emerging markets can offer higher growth potential
  with higher risk.
- **American Depositary Receipts (ADRs):** foreign company shares traded on U.S. exchanges.

## Extra risks
- **Currency risk:** returns change when exchange rates move; a stronger dollar reduces the value of
  foreign investments in dollar terms.
- **Political and economic risk:** less stable governments, different regulation.
- **Different accounting and disclosure standards,** less information, and sometimes lower liquidity.
- **Higher costs** in some funds and markets.

## Home bias
Investors everywhere tend to over-invest in their own country. International diversification is
a way to counter that bias, though how much to hold is a personal choice.
