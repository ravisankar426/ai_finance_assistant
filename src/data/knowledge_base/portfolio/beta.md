# Beta — Sensitivity to the Market

## What beta measures
Beta measures how much an investment tends to move relative to a benchmark, usually a broad market
index such as the S&P 500.
- **Beta = 1:** moves roughly in line with the market.
- **Beta > 1:** tends to swing more than the market (a beta of 1.2 suggests about a 12% move when the
  market moves 10%).
- **Beta < 1:** tends to swing less.
- **Beta near 0 or negative:** little relationship, or moves opposite to the market.

## How it's calculated
Beta = covariance of the investment's returns with the market's returns ÷ variance of the market's
returns, typically using a few years of daily, weekly, or monthly returns.

## What beta does and doesn't tell you
- It captures **market (systematic) risk** — the part diversification can't remove.
- It does **not** capture company-specific risk; a stock can have a low beta and still collapse on
  its own news.
- It is backward-looking and can change over time.

## Portfolio beta
A portfolio's beta is the weighted average of its holdings' betas. This assistant reports portfolio
beta versus SPY (an S&P 500 ETF) to show how market-sensitive a portfolio has been.
