# Volatility, Standard Deviation, and Drawdown

## Volatility
Volatility describes how much and how quickly an investment's price swings. Higher volatility means
a wider range of possible outcomes over short periods — larger gains and larger losses.

## Standard deviation
The most common volatility measure is the **standard deviation of returns**: how far returns
typically stray from their average. It is usually **annualized** (for daily returns, multiply by the
square root of about 252 trading days). A fund with 20% annualized volatility has historically had
much wider yearly swings than one with 5%.

## Maximum drawdown
**Drawdown** is the decline from a previous peak to a later low. **Maximum drawdown** is the largest
such fall over a period — it answers "how bad did it get?" Losses also need bigger gains to recover:
a 30% fall (100 → 70) needs a **43%** gain to get back to 100.

## Using these measures
- Compare investments with similar goals: two funds with the same return but different volatility
  have taken very different risks.
- Past volatility doesn't guarantee future volatility; markets can change regime quickly.
- Volatility is not the same as permanent loss, but it can lead to losses if you must sell at a
  bad time.
