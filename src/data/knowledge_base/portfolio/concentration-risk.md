# Concentration Risk

## What it is
Concentration risk is the danger of having a large share of your money in one investment, one
company, one industry, or one type of asset. If that one thing does badly, your whole portfolio
suffers.

## Common ways it happens
- A single stock grows until it dominates the portfolio.
- Holding a lot of your **employer's stock** — your job and your savings then depend on the same
  company.
- Several funds that seem different but hold the same large companies.
- Heavy exposure to one **sector**, such as technology.

## How to spot it
Look at each holding's **weight** (its value ÷ total portfolio value) and your exposure by sector.
This assistant's portfolio analysis flags any single holding above **25%** of the portfolio and any
sector above **40%** — educational thresholds, not rules.

A summary measure is the **Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)**: the sum of squared weights. A
portfolio split equally across 10 holdings has an HHI of 0.10; one dominated by a single holding
approaches 1.0.

## Ways investors reduce it
- Use broad index funds for diversification.
- Direct new contributions to underweight areas.
- Sell gradually over time, considering taxes.

## Trade-off
Concentration can produce big gains when a bet works, but it also creates the risk of large,
permanent losses. Diversification aims for steadier outcomes.
