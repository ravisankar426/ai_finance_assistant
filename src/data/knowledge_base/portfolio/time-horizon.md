# Time Horizon and Investing

## What a time horizon is
Your time horizon is how long until you need the money for a goal: months for a vacation, a few
years for a home down payment, decades for retirement.

## Why it shapes your mix
Stock prices can fall sharply in any single year, but over long periods markets have historically
recovered and grown. A long horizon gives you time to ride out downturns, so long-term goals can
usually hold more stocks. Money needed soon is usually kept in safer assets, because a drop right
before you need it may not have time to recover.

## Rough guide by horizon
- **Under ~3 years:** savings accounts, CDs, money market funds, short-term bonds.
- **~3–10 years:** a blend of bonds and stocks, often more conservative as the date approaches.
- **10+ years:** typically a larger stock allocation for growth, with some diversification.

These are general educational ranges, not recommendations.

## Several goals, several horizons
You might have an emergency fund (no horizon — always ready), a house fund (5 years), and retirement
(30 years). Each can have its own allocation.

## As the goal approaches
Many investors gradually shift toward lower-risk assets as a goal nears — the idea behind target
date funds' glide paths.
