# Understanding Your Risk Tolerance

## Two sides of risk tolerance
- **Risk capacity (financial):** how much loss you can afford without derailing your goals. It
  depends on your time horizon, income stability, savings, and obligations.
- **Risk attitude (emotional):** how much volatility you can live with without panicking and
  selling at the wrong time.

A sound plan respects the **lower** of the two.

## Questions that reveal your tolerance
- When do you need this money?
- If your portfolio fell 20% in a few months, would you hold, buy more, or sell?
- How stable is your income, and do you have an emergency fund?
- How much investing experience do you have?

## Common risk profiles
- **Conservative:** prioritizes stability; mostly bonds and cash, smaller stock allocation.
- **Moderate:** balances growth and stability; a mix of stocks and bonds.
- **Aggressive:** prioritizes long-term growth; mostly stocks, accepting large swings.

These labels are starting points, not rules — and this assistant uses them only for education.

## Why it matters
Investors who take more risk than they can stomach often sell after a big drop, locking in losses
and missing the recovery. A mix you can stick with through downturns usually beats a "better" mix
you abandon.

## Revisit it
Risk tolerance changes with age, wealth, goals, and life events. Review it periodically.
