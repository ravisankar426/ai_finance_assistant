# Short-Term vs. Long-Term Goals

## Saving vs. investing
- **Saving** means putting money in safe, accessible places — bank savings accounts, CDs, money
  market funds — where it won't lose value. Best for money you'll need soon.
- **Investing** means buying assets like stocks and bonds that can grow more over time but can also
  lose value. Best for money you won't need for many years.

## Matching money to goals
- **Short-term (roughly under 3 years):** emergency fund, a car, a vacation, a near-term down payment.
  Prioritize safety and access.
- **Medium-term (about 3–10 years):** a home purchase in several years, a child's education. Often a
  balance of safety and growth.
- **Long-term (10+ years):** retirement. Growth matters most, and time allows recovery from
  downturns.

## Why the distinction matters
Putting short-term money in stocks risks having to sell after a drop. Keeping long-term money only in
cash risks losing purchasing power to inflation over decades.

## Building wealth over time
Controlling high-interest debt, keeping an emergency fund, and investing a portion of each paycheck
for long-term goals are steady habits that build financial security.
