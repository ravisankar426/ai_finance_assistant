# Employer 401(k) Matching

## What a match is
Many employers contribute to your workplace retirement plan when you contribute — a **match**. A
common formula is "50 cents for every dollar you contribute, up to 6% of your salary." If you earn
$60,000 and contribute 6% ($3,600), the employer adds $1,800.

## Why it matters
An employer match is effectively an immediate return on your contribution — a 50% match is a 50%
return before any investment growth. Few other financial decisions offer that, which is why
educators often suggest contributing at least enough to get the full match, even while paying down
other debts.

## Things to check in your plan
- **The formula:** dollar-for-dollar vs. partial match, and the cap.
- **Vesting:** employer contributions may "vest" over several years; if you leave before you're fully
  vested, you can lose some of the employer money. Your own contributions are always yours.
- **Timing:** some employers match each paycheck; others once a year. Front-loading contributions can
  sometimes miss part of a per-paycheck match.
- **Automatic enrollment and increases:** many plans enroll you automatically at a default rate;
  check that it's high enough to capture the full match.

## Where to find details
Your plan's Summary Plan Description explains the match, vesting schedule, and investment options.
