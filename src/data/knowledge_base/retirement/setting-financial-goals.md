# Setting Financial Goals

## Make goals specific
A useful goal answers **what**, **how much**, and **by when**: "Save $15,000 for a home down payment
in 4 years" is actionable; "save more" isn't.

## Turn a goal into a monthly plan
1. **Target amount** — in today's dollars, adjusted for inflation if the goal is far away.
2. **Time horizon** — months or years until you need the money.
3. **Starting point** — what you've already saved.
4. **Monthly contribution** — what you need to set aside each month, considering expected returns
   for the way you'll invest it.

For short-term goals, expected returns are small, so the plan is mostly simple division. For long
horizons, compound growth does more of the work — which is why the required monthly amount drops
the earlier you start.

## Match the investment to the horizon
Short-term goals usually belong in safe, accessible accounts; long-term goals can use diversified
investments with growth potential. Your risk tolerance matters too.

## If the plan doesn't fit your budget
Adjust a lever: extend the deadline, lower the target, increase contributions, or accept a
different (riskier or safer) investment mix — understanding the trade-offs.

## Track and review
Automate contributions, check progress a few times a year, and revise when life changes.
