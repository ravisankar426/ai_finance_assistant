# Making Retirement Savings Last

## The challenge
In retirement you shift from saving to spending. The key risk is **outliving your money**: you don't
know how long you'll live, what returns markets will deliver, or how inflation will behave.

## The "4% rule" — a rule of thumb
A well-known heuristic says that withdrawing about **4% of your savings in the first year**, then
adjusting that dollar amount for inflation each year, historically lasted about 30 years in many U.S.
market periods. With $1,000,000 saved, 4% is **$40,000** in the first year.

Important caveats: it's based on historical data, assumes a particular stock/bond mix, ignores taxes
and fees, and may be too high or too low for a given person or market environment.

## Risks to plan for
- **Sequence-of-returns risk:** large losses early in retirement hurt more than the same losses
  later, because withdrawals lock them in.
- **Inflation:** rising prices erode fixed income over decades.
- **Longevity:** planning only to your average life expectancy leaves a significant chance of
  living longer.
- **Health care costs.**

## Common strategies
- Keep a cash buffer for a year or two of spending to avoid selling investments after a drop.
- Adjust withdrawals flexibly after poor market years.
- Consider delaying Social Security for a larger, inflation-adjusted lifetime benefit.
- Some retirees use annuities to cover essential expenses with guaranteed income.
