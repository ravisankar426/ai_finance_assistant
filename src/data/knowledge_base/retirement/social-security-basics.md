# Social Security Retirement Benefits

## How you qualify
Workers earn Social Security credits by paying Social Security taxes on their earnings; most people
need about 10 years of work to qualify for retirement benefits. Your benefit is based on your
highest-earning years.

## Full retirement age
Your **full retirement age (FRA)** depends on your birth year. For people born in **1960 or later,
full retirement age is 67**.

## Claiming early or late
- **Earliest age: 62.** Claiming before FRA permanently reduces your monthly benefit. For someone with
  an FRA of 67, claiming at 62 reduces the benefit by about **30%**.
- **Delaying past FRA** earns **delayed retirement credits** — about **8% per year** — until age **70**,
  after which there's no further increase.

## Choosing when to claim
Factors include your health and life expectancy, other income and savings, whether you're still
working, and benefits for a spouse. Delaying generally means a larger lifetime monthly benefit,
while claiming early provides income sooner.

## Plan with your own numbers
Create a *my Social Security* account at ssa.gov to see your earnings record and personalized benefit
estimates at different claiming ages.

## A reminder
Social Security was designed to replace only part of pre-retirement income; personal savings and
workplace plans usually fill the rest.
