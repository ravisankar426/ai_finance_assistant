# Retirement Planning Basics

## Start early
Time is the biggest advantage in retirement saving: money invested in your 20s has decades to
compound. Starting early also means smaller contributions can reach the same goal.

## Where retirement money usually comes from
- **Workplace plans** such as 401(k) and 403(b) plans, often with an employer match.
- **Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs)** — traditional and Roth — which anyone with earned income
  can generally open.
- **Social Security** retirement benefits.
- Personal savings, pensions (less common today), and other assets.

## The basic steps
1. **Join your workplace plan** if one is offered, and contribute at least enough to get the full
   employer match.
2. **Choose investments** that fit your time horizon — many plans offer target date funds.
3. **Increase contributions** over time, for example with each raise.
4. **Avoid early withdrawals:** taking money out before age 59½ usually triggers taxes plus a 10%
   additional tax, and loses future growth.
5. **Review periodically** — your allocation, fees, and whether you're on track.

## Tax advantages
Retirement accounts are either **tax-deferred** (traditional: deduct or exclude contributions now,
pay tax on withdrawals) or **tax-free on qualified withdrawals** (Roth: pay tax now). See the Tax
Education articles for details and current limits.
