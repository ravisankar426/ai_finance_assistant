# Saving for College

## Start with a goal and a timeline
College is a goal with a fixed date. Estimate costs (tuition, room and board, books) for the type of
school you have in mind, and the years until enrollment. Even partial savings reduce future
borrowing.

## Common ways to save
- **529 plans:** tax-advantaged education savings plans sponsored by states. Investment growth is
  tax-free when used for qualified education expenses. Many plans offer age-based portfolios that
  become more conservative as the student nears college. See the Tax Education article on 529 plans.
- **Prepaid tuition plans:** some states let you prepay future tuition at participating schools.
- **Regular savings or brokerage accounts:** flexible, but without the tax benefits.

## Investment approach
Because the date is fixed, many savers hold more stocks when the child is young and shift toward
bonds and cash as college approaches — the same idea as a target date fund.

## Balancing priorities
Many educators suggest not sacrificing retirement savings for college savings: students can borrow
for college, but there are no loans for retirement.

## Financial aid
How savings affect financial aid depends on who owns the account and the aid formula; check current
rules when planning.
