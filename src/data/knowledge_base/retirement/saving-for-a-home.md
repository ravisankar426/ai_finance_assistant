# Saving for a Home Down Payment

## How big a down payment
The down payment is the part of the home price you pay upfront. Many loans allow small down
payments, but with a **conventional loan, putting down less than 20%** usually means paying **private
mortgage insurance (PMI)**, which raises your monthly cost. Some government-backed loans allow lower
down payments with their own insurance or fees.

## Costs beyond the down payment
- **Closing costs** (loan fees, title, appraisal, and more).
- Moving costs, furnishings, and immediate repairs.
- An emergency fund that's still intact after you buy.

## Where to keep house money
A down payment needed within a few years is a **short-term goal**. Because a market drop right
before you buy may not recover in time, savings accounts, CDs, money market funds, or short-term
Treasury securities are common choices rather than stocks.

## Building the fund
1. Estimate a target: price range × down payment percentage + closing costs.
2. Divide by the months until your target date to get a monthly savings amount.
3. Automate transfers and track progress.

## Before buying
Compare loan offers from several lenders, check your credit, and use budgeting tools to make sure the
full monthly cost — mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance — fits your budget.
