# Annuities

## What an annuity is
An annuity is a contract with an insurance company: you pay money (a lump sum or a series of
payments) and the insurer promises payments to you, either right away or starting later. Annuities
are often used to create a steady income stream in retirement.

## Main types
- **Fixed annuities:** pay a guaranteed interest rate or fixed payments.
- **Variable annuities:** payments depend on the performance of investment options (subaccounts)
  you choose, so they can rise or fall.
- **Indexed annuities:** returns are tied to a market index, usually with limits (caps) on gains and
  some protection against losses.
- **Immediate vs. deferred:** income can start right away or at a future date.

## Potential benefits
- Income you can't outlive (with lifetime payout options).
- Tax-deferred growth.
- Optional guarantees, such as death benefits or minimum income riders.

## Costs and drawbacks
- Fees can be high: mortality and expense charges, administrative fees, fund expenses, and fees for
  riders.
- **Surrender charges** if you withdraw money in the first several years.
- Withdrawals before age 59½ may face a 10% additional federal tax.
- Guarantees depend on the insurer's financial strength.

## Before buying
Annuities are complex. Read the contract and prospectus, compare costs, and understand how the seller
is compensated.
